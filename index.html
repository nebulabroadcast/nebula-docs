    Nebula Broadcast Automation & MAM

    Nebula is a professional, open-source media asset management (MAM) and broadcast automation system engineered from the ground up for demanding, 24/7 broadcast environments.

    It serves as the central orchestrator for media assets, scheduling, and playout control, offering broadcast operators a unified, lightning-fast workflow.

    What Nebula Can Do

    Everything below happens in one web interface, which updates live as colleagues and background services change things.

    Manage media

    • Find anything fast: Browse the library in tabs, with thumbnails if you like, narrow it down with search and filters, and keep the combinations you use often as saved views.
    • Describe it your way: Folders give each kind of content (movies, episodes, commercials, jingles...) its own metadata form, and links between folders connect related assets, such as a series and its episodes.
    • Preview and trim in the browser: Play a proxy of any asset with frame accuracy, trim it, mark sub-clips and pick a poster frame, all from the keyboard if you like.
    • Transcription (coming soon): Automatic speech-to-text for your assets, so you can search what was said and select a passage straight from the transcript.

    Get media in

    • Watch folders: Files dropped on a share become assets automatically, with their technical metadata filled in.
    • Upload and import: Upload from the browser or let the import service transcode incoming files to your house format.
    • Background jobs: Actions create proxies, transcode and deliver media without anyone clicking, and the jobs monitor shows their progress.

    Schedule and play out

    • Plan the week: Lay out programme blocks in the scheduler calendar, by hand or from a scheduling template.
    • Build the rundown: Drag assets from the browser into the rundown. Use placeholders for media that has not arrived yet, add live events and lead-ins and lead-outs.
    • Go on air: Control the channel with the on-air controls, and decide per event and per item whether it starts automatically or waits for the operator.
    • Know what aired: Every played item lands in the as-run log.
    • Playout engines: Production playout runs on CasparCG. Conti, a lightweight software engine, is also available, but it is meant for testing only.

    Fit it to your station

    • Configuration as code: Channels, folders, metadata fields and services are defined in settings files you can keep under version control.
    • Your own rules: Validators check and complete metadata whenever an asset is saved.
    • Scale out: Workers on several machines share transcoding, metadata extraction and playout control.
    • Open source and open API: Everything the interface does goes through a documented HTTP API, so other systems can do it too.
